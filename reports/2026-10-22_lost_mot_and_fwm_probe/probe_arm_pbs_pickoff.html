Two-PBS pick-off for the phase conjugate

A single λ/4 + PBS sends the conjugate straight back to the laser. Putting a polariser (PBS B) between the pick-off cube (PBS A) and the atoms fixes that. PBS B projects the probe, so the conjugate can't undo the λ/2 on the way back. It reaches PBS A at 45°, and half of it leaves through the fourth port onto the detector. Plain reflections from the cell side come back crossed and are dumped at PBS B.

Why PBS B matters. Without it, the conjugate retraces every reciprocal element. It arrives at PBS A in the probe's own state and passes straight through. PBS B discards half the probe on the way in, which breaks that symmetry. That half isn't wasted: it goes to PD ref and normalises the probe power.

Alignment

  1. Start from a working punch-through arm. The probe must already overlap the cloud.
  2. Insert PBS B and the λ/2. Find the λ/2's zero, where PD ref reads its minimum. Then rotate it 22.5°, so PD ref matches the transmitted power.
  3. Insert the ND and PBS A with the same orientation as PBS B. Use the power λ/2 to set 20 µW after PBS A, measured with a power meter.
  4. Align PD FWM with a stand-in signal. With the MOT off, put a temporary mirror after the cell, normal to the probe, with a spare λ/4 in front of it. The extra λ/4 gives the return the conjugate's helicity, so it follows the signal path. About 5 µW then leaves PBS A's fourth port. Place the lens, pinhole and DET36A/M on it, with an ND in front for now. Then remove the mirror.
  5. Measure the floor. With the probe on and no atoms, PD FWM reads only leaking reflections. Tilt the λ/4, the λ/2 and PBS B to bring it down. With the probe blocked, it reads fluorescence and pump scatter.
  6. Look for the signal. Run the probe window after the load and step imaging_push_aom across 83.5 ± 3 MHz. Use the four-way subtraction (probe on/off × atoms on/off) and the checks in the evening report.

Uses the same arm as the punch-through sheet. The far-side photodiode is on its own sheet.
