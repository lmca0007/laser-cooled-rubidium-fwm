A photodiode on the far side of the cell

The probe leaves the cell on the side opposite the arm, 39 mm off the MOT beam axis at the retro optics. A pick-off mirror and a photodiode there are the cheapest addition to the punch-through arm. This sheet covers what that detector sees and what it can't.

It doesn't make the conjugate easier to separate, because the conjugate never gets there. With counter-propagating pumps, phase matching sends the conjugate exactly back along the probe. The far side sees only the transmitted probe.

It is still worth building, and building first. It is easy to align, it measures the cloud's optical depth along the probe, and it records the pump–probe spectrum, which comes from the same χ(3) process. Four-wave mixing shows up there as a small gain on the probe. Proof of the conjugate still needs the backward pick-off on the arm.

What the far-side detector measures

With a 10 µW probe on the DET36A/M at 100 kΩ (about 50 mV/µW, about 40 kHz), the photodiode reads about 0.5 V before any atoms. Divide by PD ref in every sample: drifts in the probe power are larger than the four-wave-mixing signal.

The two use the same arm and the same probe window, so both can record in every shot. Run the far side first to confirm the optical depth and the spectrum. Then align PBS A and look for the conjugate where the spectrum says it should be.

Steps

  1. Start from the punch-through arm, with the probe overlapping the cloud. Add the reflective ND, PBS B and PD ref so the probe is about 10 µW and normalised.
  2. Place M3 at about (+280, +39) mm, before the retro λ/4. Fold the probe away from the MOT beam. Check with a card that its mount doesn't touch the retro beam.
  3. Add the lens, the iris at its focus and the DET36A/M with a 100 kΩ load. The iris cuts most MOT fluorescence and pump scatter.
  4. Measure the optical depth. Set the probe to about 90 MHz and compare probe transmission with and without the MOT.
  5. Record the pump–probe spectrum. In one shot, ramp imaging_push_aom across 83.5 ± 1 MHz over a few ms. Record PD trans and PD ref on two AnalogIn channels. Take a matching shot with no atoms and divide.
  6. Then add the pick-off from the pick-off sheet and look for the conjugate at the frequency where the spectrum shows gain.
