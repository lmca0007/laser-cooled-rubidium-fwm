krb2 · stage 1 of the FWM probe · Rb-87 D2, 780.24 nm

Punch-through probe at the octagon cell

The first build sends the 200 mW side-imaging beam through the MOT at 8° to MOT beam 1-5. If the MOT is depleted when the probe is on, the probe overlaps the cloud. Everything placed here stays in the final FWM arm. The faded parts are added in stage 2.

Parts

Table frame of optics/fwm_probe/geometry.py: cloud at the origin, +x along 1→5, +y along 3→7, in mm. The positions are suggestions; move them to suit the table, but keep each optic inside the wedge.

5Dump or DET36A/M(+280, +39.4)Stops the transmitted probe before the retro λ/4. If you put the DET36A/M here with a 100 kΩ load (about 50 mV/µW), you can see the MOT absorb a weak probe.
6Stage 2: glass plate in place of M1(−280, −200)An uncoated or wedged window at 45°, s-polarised. It reflects about 9% of the probe up the arm and transmits about 80% of the returning signal to the DET36A/M behind it (1 MΩ load, iris in front).
7Stage 2: λ/2, λ/4on the armMake the probe circular with σ opposite to F. No lens: the 2 mm beam already roughly matches the cloud.

Intensities. 1 mW in a 2 mm beam is about 64 mW/cm². That is about 5 times the intensity of one 25 mW MOT beam and gives a strong push. The PBS minimum of 0.2 mW is about equal to one MOT beam.

Procedure

  1. Block the beam and put on goggles. 200 mW at 780 nm is class 3B and invisible. Set the λ/2 so the PBS transmits 1 mW, measured with a power meter.
  2. Set the height. Iris the beam to about 2 mm. Use M1 and M2 to bring it level at the height of the MOT beam centre, checked at two points along the arm.
  3. Aim through the cell centre. At 8° the probe passes 6.8 mm from the MOT beam centre at face 1 and 6.8 mm on the other side at face 5, at the same height. Equal offsets on both faces mean it crosses the cloud. Check with a card.
  4. Dump the exit beam at about (+280, +39), before the retro λ/4. Check that nothing goes back into the retro optics.
  5. Open the probe during the load. During the load imaging_push_aom is already on at the push frequency, which is near resonance, so opening side_imaging_shutter for the last 0.5–1 s of the load sends near-resonant light into the MOT. This needs a small sequence change. Alternate shots with the probe on and off, and compare the side fluorescence images.
  6. Search, then refine. If the MOT is unchanged, step M2 in small raster steps and raise the power. Once the MOT shrinks or moves, lower the power until it is only partly depleted. Then walk M1 and M2 for the largest depletion. That centres the probe on the cloud.
  7. Optional absorption check. With the DET36A/M at the exit, a weak probe of a few µW shows a dip when the MOT is present. This confirms the overlap on a single detector, without the camera.
